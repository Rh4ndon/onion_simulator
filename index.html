How the model works

Each simulated day, the app combines soil pH and the iron you set to work out how much iron the onion can actually use. It does not judge iron from pH alone.

  • Effective Fe = Fe concentration × pH factor. The pH factor falls smoothly as soil gets more alkaline (about 1.1 at pH 5, 0.56 at pH 7, 0.27 at pH 7.9, 0.08 at pH 9).
  • Chlorophyll rises with effective Fe until it levels off near 120 to 150 ppm. Very high effective Fe (above about 200 ppm) lowers chlorophyll slightly and risks toxicity. Chlorophyll moves toward its target over several days, so a fix takes time to show.
  • Growth each day is scaled by that day's chlorophyll. Height, leaf number and bulb size are added up day by day, so pausing and changing pH or Fe changes only the days that follow.
  • Very acidic soil (below about pH 5.5) slows growth even though iron is plentiful, because of other nutrient problems and metal toxicity. Lower pH is not always better.
  • Stages scale with total duration. Transplanting: establishment, vegetative, bulbing (from 50%), maturity (from 85%). Direct seeding: germination (first 10 days), seedling, vegetative, bulbing (from 55%), maturity (from 85%).

The thresholds (for example, "deficient" below about 60 ppm effective Fe) are a teaching model tuned to match the direction and size of the published results. They are not measured cutoffs. Use soil tests and your local agricultural technician for real fertilizer decisions.
